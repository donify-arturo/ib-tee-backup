## [ ] S1.2-01 · Nuclear symbol: mass number on top, and counting particles in atoms and ions
Priority 1 · S1.2.1 · Highest-value card: 50% correct on May 2025 Paper 2, and the May 2025 report lists atomic mass and number annotation as difficult. The same inversion appears in legacy reports.

**Step by step** (`25M.2.HL.6215.2`, 1 marks)

Thallium is a group 13 metal. 30% of its atoms have 122 neutrons and the other atoms have 124 neutrons. Part (a)(i): using section 7 of the data booklet, write the nuclear symbol of the isotope with 122 neutrons. [1]

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 5 s): Deduce: use given data; one mark for the final symbol.
   - *command term*: Deduce
   - *demands*: Reach the symbol from the neutron count and the atomic number in section 7.
   - *score mechanics*: One mark, all or nothing, for the complete symbol.
   - *avoid*: Writing a calculation and leaving the symbol box unfinished.
   - *why*: Deduce means reaching a conclusion from information given. The information is the neutron count and the booklet's atomic number.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): Read Z from section 7, then build this isotope's symbol.
   - *ao level*: AO3
   - *syllabus hook*: S1.2.1: use the nuclear symbol to deduce protons, neutrons and electrons in atoms and ions.
   - *textbook gap*: Books go from symbol to counts. This goes from a neutron count to the symbol, with Z taken from the booklet.
   - *avoid*: Treating it as an easy recall mark: about half lost it (25M.2.HL.6215.2).
   - *why*: S1.2.1 expects you to move between symbol and particle counts. Here you go backwards, from neutrons to symbol, so you must know the layout.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): You get neutrons, not a mass number, so you must add.
   - *disguise*: It is written as an abundance story about a heavy metal, with the neutron count given instead of a mass number.
   - *heuristic*: Top counts nucleons, so it is whole. Bottom counts protons, so it fixes the element.
   - *avoid*: Using the 30% figure or the booklet's Ar as the top number.
   - *why*: The stem hides A. Adding protons from the table to the neutrons given rebuilds it, which is the only real step in this mark.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 10 s): Rule out decimals on top and any top below bottom.
   - *definitely wrong*: A top number smaller than the bottom number: Mass number includes every proton, so it cannot be below atomic number.; A decimal top number such as 204.38: That is an average Ar. Mass number counts particles, so it is whole.; A bottom number that changes between isotopes: All thallium atoms have the same number of protons.
   - *avoid*: Using 204.38 as a mass number; report 03M.2.SL.TZ0.5 saw the same slip with 12.01.
   - *why*: A counts whole particles, so it cannot be a decimal. A includes all the protons, so it can never be smaller than Z.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 10 s): Section 7 shows Z above; the symbol wants Z below.
   - *sections*: 7
   - *pattern*: The key in section 7 reads atomic number, element, relative atomic mass, from top to bottom.
   - *memorise*: Nuclear symbol layout: mass number on top, atomic number below. This layout is not in the section 7 text.
   - *avoid*: Copying the booklet's order of numbers straight into the symbol.
   - *why*: Section 7 shows atomic number at the top and relative atomic mass below. The nuclear symbol uses the opposite order, and the report suggests this causes inversion.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 20 s): Twenty seconds: look up, add, write. Then move on.
   - *workflow*: Z from the table; Add neutrons for A; A on top, Z below
   - *fat to trim*: Skip the abundance data; it belongs to the next part.
   - *avoid*: Working out the average mass before finishing this part.
   - *why*: This is one mark with one addition. Spending time here only cuts into the two-mark calculation that follows.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 15 s): One mark: 203 on top, 81 below, beside Tl.
   - *trigger phrases*: 203 over 81, beside Tl
   - *trap boundary*: An inverted symbol has the right numbers but does not earn the mark.
   - *delta*: No partial credit: one mark for the whole symbol, with both numbers in position.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.2.HL.6215.2
   - *avoid*: Writing 81 over 203, the error reported (25M.2.HL.6215.2).
   - *why*: The mark needs both numbers in the right place. The report says about half inverted them, possibly because of how section 7 lays out its numbers.

**Answer:** The symbol has 203 on top and 81 below, to the left of Tl (²⁰³₈₁Tl). 81 protons + 122 neutrons = 203. (Mass number (protons + neutrons) goes on top; atomic number goes below.)

## [ ] S1.2-02 · Neutrons add mass but no charge: field deflection and isotope properties
Priority 2 · S1.2.1, S1.2.2 · The field MCQ has no reported percentage. The priority comes from isotope properties: the May 2025 and May 2019 reports list them as difficult, and definitions were repeatedly flagged. Reports disagree on whether mass counts, so give density or boiling point.

**Step by step** (`25M.1A.HL.6207.1`, 1 marks)

A beam of protons, neutrons and electrons passes between two electrodes, positive at the top and negative at the bottom. The diagram shows three paths: x bends towards the positive electrode, y carries straight on and z bends towards the negative electrode. Which row identifies the particles, in the order protons, electrons, neutrons?
A. y, z, x
B. x, y, z
C. z, x, y
D. x, z, y

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 5 s): MCQ stem: pick the one correct row of three identities.
   - *command term*: none
   - *demands*: Match all three particles to paths in a single option row.
   - *score mechanics*: One mark, all or nothing, for the correct letter.
   - *avoid*: Marking a row after checking only the neutron column.
   - *why*: All three identities must be right in one row. Fixing two paths also fixes the third, so a third check is optional.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): Link each particle's charge to the path it takes.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: S1.2.1: nucleus of protons and neutrons, electrons outside; relative masses and charges should be known.
   - *textbook gap*: Books give a table of charges and masses. This question makes you apply them to movement in an electric field.
   - *avoid*: Reading the option columns as protons, neutrons, electrons; electrons come second here.
   - *why*: S1.2.1 expects the relative charges to be known. The diagram tests whether you can use them, rather than just recite them.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): A physics-style diagram hides a simple table of charges.
   - *disguise*: It shows a beam and electrodes rather than asking for charges, and the table uses letters rather than particle names.
   - *heuristic*: Ask what each particle's charge makes it do. No charge means no bending.
   - *avoid*: Thinking about particle speed or size; only the direction of each path is asked.
   - *why*: Electrodes look like physics, but only the sign of each particle matters. The same no-charge idea explains why neutrons do not change electron attraction.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 10 s): A and B bend neutrons; D sends protons to positive.
   - *definitely wrong*: A: It puts neutrons on x, but an uncharged particle cannot bend.; B: It puts neutrons on z, but an uncharged particle cannot bend.; D: It sends positive protons towards the positive electrode, where they would be repelled.
   - *avoid*: Keeping an option where the neutron path bends.
   - *why*: A neutral particle cannot bend, which removes A and B. Like charges repel, which removes D. No other reasoning is needed.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 5 s): No booklet needed: know relative charges by heart.
   - *sections*: 
   - *pattern*: Nothing to read here. The sign of each charge is enough.
   - *memorise*: Relative charges: proton +1, electron −1, neutron 0. Relative masses: proton and neutron about 1, electron negligible.
   - *avoid*: Hunting through the booklet for particle data during this question.
   - *why*: The syllabus says relative masses and charges should be known. Only the actual values are in the booklet, and they are not needed for a direction.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 20 s): Fix the neutron, check the proton, answer.
   - *workflow*: Neutron goes straight; Proton to negative plate; Pick the row
   - *fat to trim*: Skip the sizes of the deflections and the particle masses; only direction matters.
   - *avoid*: Checking all three particles against all four options.
   - *why*: The neutron removes two options straight away, and one more check separates the two left, so the whole question takes seconds.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 10 s): One mark for protons z, electrons x, neutrons y.
   - *trigger phrases*: Correct answer: C
   - *trap boundary*: C and D both have neutrons on y; only the direction of the proton separates them.
   - *delta*: D swaps protons and electrons. C sends the positive proton to the negative electrode, which is the right attraction.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.1A.HL.6207.1
   - *avoid*: Choosing D, which sends protons towards the positive electrode.
   - *why*: Each charge fixes one path, so only one row of the table can be right. There is nothing to calculate and no partial credit.

**Answer:** C. Protons follow z, electrons follow x and neutrons follow y. (Only charge bends a path: neutrons go straight and opposite charges attract.)
**Try next:** `19M.2.HL.TZ2.3`, `19M.2.SL.TZ2.3`, `21N.2.HL.TZ0.9`

## [ ] S1.2-04 · Calculate Ar from given abundances: never copy the booklet value, and obey the decimal places
Priority 2 · S1.2.2 · Both 2025 Paper 2 markschemes carry the same rejection note. The method is listed as well prepared in many sessions, so marks are lost at the finish rather than in the arithmetic.

**Step by step** (`25M.2.HL.6215.2`, 2 marks)

Use the thallium data again: 30% of the atoms have 122 neutrons and the other atoms have 124 neutrons. Part (a)(ii): calculate the relative atomic mass of thallium, giving two decimal places. [2]

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 5 s): Calculate: show the stages; the precise value earns M2.
   - *command term*: Calculate
   - *demands*: Show the weighted sum, then give the value to two decimal places.
   - *score mechanics*: M1 for working, M2 for the value. A correct final answer alone earns both.
   - *avoid*: Writing only a final number that may be wrong and could lose both marks.
   - *why*: Calculate means a numerical answer with relevant stages. Working protects M1 if the final value slips.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): Weighted mean, after turning neutron counts into mass numbers.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: S1.2.2: calculations involving non-integer relative atomic masses and isotope abundances from given data.
   - *textbook gap*: Books give mass numbers and percentages directly. Here you must find the masses from neutrons and Z, and the booklet value is a rejected answer.
   - *avoid*: Writing the booklet Ar after a correct calculation, as reported (25M.2.HL.6215.2).
   - *why*: S1.2.2 wants non-integer Ar from given data. The booklet holds Ar for the natural element, while this question sets its own abundances.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): Neutron counts and 'the remainder' hide the usual data.
   - *disguise*: It gives neutrons rather than mass numbers, one abundance rather than two, and sits in a question about a heavy metal.
   - *heuristic*: Ar needs masses and fractions. If either is missing, rebuild it first: protons + neutrons, and 100 minus the given percentage.
   - *avoid*: Multiplying 122 and 124 by the abundances; those are neutrons, not masses.
   - *why*: Both masses and one abundance are missing from the stem. Rebuilding them is two quick steps, and then it is the familiar weighted mean.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 10 s): Rule out anything outside 203 to 205, or below 204.
   - *definitely wrong*: A value outside 203 to 205: A weighted mean always lies between the isotope masses.; A value of 204 or lower: The heavier isotope is the more abundant, so the mean must be above the midpoint.; 204.38: This is the booklet value, which the markscheme does not accept for M2.; A value with units: Relative atomic mass has no units; reports flag grams as an error (08M.2.SL.TZ0.2).
   - *avoid*: Accepting a plain midpoint of 204, which ignores the abundances.
   - *why*: A weighted mean lies between its masses. With 70% heavy atoms, it must be above the midpoint, so these answers can be rejected on sight.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 10 s): Take only Z from section 7, not its Ar.
   - *sections*: 7
   - *pattern*: Section 7 key: atomic number on top, relative atomic mass below. Use the top number only.
   - *memorise*: The weighted-mean calculation is not in the booklet.
   - *avoid*: Using section 7's Ar as your final answer (25M.2.HL.6215.2).
   - *why*: Section 7 sits right next to the answer line in your mind, but its Ar is for the natural element. The markscheme refuses it here.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 45 s): One line of working, one exact answer, then stop.
   - *workflow*: Protons + neutrons = mass; Remainder gives abundance; Weighted sum; Obey decimal places
   - *fat to trim*: Skip units and any comment on why your value differs from the booklet.
   - *avoid*: Looking up thallium's Ar to compare it with your answer.
   - *why*: Two marks need one line. The shortcut 203 + 2 × 0.70 lets you check it in seconds.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 15 s): M1 for the weighted sum; M2 for exactly 204.40.
   - *trigger phrases*: (0.3 × 203) + (0.7 × 205); 204.40
   - *trap boundary*: 204.38 does not earn M2, even after a correct setup.
   - *delta*: Partial: correct setup, then a wrong precision or the booklet value. Full: the setup and 204.40. A correct final answer alone earns both marks.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.2.HL.6215.2
   - *avoid*: Giving 204.38 from the booklet, or dropping the last zero (25M.2.HL.6215.2).
   - *why*: The second mark rewards your own value to the requested precision. The markscheme names the booklet value as not accepted for M2.

**Answer:** Ar = (0.30 × 203) + (0.70 × 205) = 204.40. (Use the question's own abundances; the booklet's Ar is never the answer here.)

## [ ] S1.2-03 · Mass spectrum scaled to tallest peak = 100: divide by the sum of heights

An element's mass spectrum has an abundance axis marked 50 and 100 and an m/z axis marked from 2 to 12. The tallest peak reaches 100. The exact peaks are not in the available text, so read them from the figure in the paper. What is the relative atomic mass of the element?
