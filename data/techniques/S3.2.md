## [ ] S3.2-01 · Name the group, not the class: hydroxyl, carbonyl, amido or amino
Priority 1 · S3.2.2 · About 50% on this part in May 2025; a related SL item fell to 27% correct, with amido misread as secondary amino.

**Step by step** (`25M.2.HL.6221.1`, 1 marks)

Phenol red, bromocresol green and phenolphthalein are acid-base indicators. The drawn structures each have three benzene rings, and each shows O-H bonded to a ring. Apart from phenyl, state a functional group all three share.

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 5 s): State: one name, with no explanation.
   - *command term*: State
   - *demands*: The name of one shared group.
   - *score mechanics*: One mark, all or nothing, for an accepted name.
   - *avoid*: Adding a second group that could contradict the first.
   - *why*: State asks for a specific name without explanation, so reasoning earns nothing extra.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): Name a shared group across three large, unfamiliar molecules.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: S3.2.2: identify functional groups by name and structure, including hydroxyl and phenyl.
   - *textbook gap*: Books label simple alcohols. This test finds the group on a ring in a large molecule and rejects the class word.
   - *avoid*: Being put off by the sulfur and bromine atoms in the drawings.
   - *why*: The molecules are large, but only one short syllabus name is tested. Size is a distraction.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): Big indicators hide a one-word functional group answer.
   - *disguise*: Three large dye structures with sulfur, bromine and several rings.
   - *heuristic*: Ignore the size; ask which atoms repeat in all three.
   - *avoid*: Looking for an unusual group because the molecules look unusual.
   - *why*: Functional groups are local, so the rest of the molecule does not change the group's name.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 5 s): Class names and ions cannot earn this mark.
   - *definitely wrong*: Alcohol or phenol: These name compound classes, not functional groups.; Hydroxide: Hydroxide is an ion; this O-H is covalently bonded.; Phenyl: The question excludes it.
   - *avoid*: Choosing carbonyl or carboxyl, seen in a few scripts, without checking all three structures.
   - *why*: The question asks for a group, so a class word or ion name fails before chemistry is checked.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 5 s): Group names are not in the booklet sections provided.
   - *sections*: 
   - *pattern*: No table replaces this recall.
   - *memorise*: Halogeno, hydroxyl, carbonyl, carboxyl, alkoxy, amino, amido, ester and phenyl are NOT in the sections provided; memorise each name and structure.
   - *avoid*: Searching the IR table for a name it was not built to give.
   - *why*: Sections 20 to 22 give spectral data by bond or proton type, not a naming list for this question.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 15 s): Spot the repeat, write one word.
   - *workflow*: Exclude phenyl; Find the repeated group; Use group name, not class
   - *fat to trim*: Skip the sulfur groups and bromines; they are not shared by all three.
   - *avoid*: Comparing every part of all three molecules.
   - *why*: This is a one-mark naming task, so the only worthwhile check is the word itself.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 5 s): Hydroxyl or hydroxy scores; alcohol, phenol and hydroxide do not.
   - *trigger phrases*: hydroxyl; hydroxy
   - *trap boundary*: Alcohol, phenol and hydroxide score zero.
   - *delta*: One word decides the mark: hydroxyl scores; alcohol, describing the same O-H, does not.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.2.HL.6221.1
   - *avoid*: Writing alcohols, the most common mistake reported in 25M.2.HL.6221.1.
   - *why*: The mark rewards the group name. Alcohol and phenol name compound classes, and hydroxide names an ion.

**Answer:** Hydroxyl (hydroxy is also accepted). (O-H is named hydroxyl, whatever class the molecule belongs to.)

## [ ] S3.2-02 · Draw enantiomers: find the centre, then reflect in wedge-dash
Priority 1 · S3.2.7 · About half succeeded in May 2025; drawing 3D isomers is flagged as difficult in several sessions.

**Step by step** (`25M.2.HL.6221.4`, 2 marks)

Substance X, CH3CHBrC(CH3)3, has a chiral centre. Draw its two optical isomers so that the 3D arrangement around that centre is clear.

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 5 s): Draw: accurate structures showing 3D orientation.
   - *command term*: Draw
   - *demands*: Two accurate 3D structures around the chiral centre.
   - *score mechanics*: Two marks for the correct 3D pair; one maximum without wedge-dash.
   - *avoid*: Describing the isomers in words.
   - *why*: Draw demands an accurate diagram, and this question specifically asks for orientation around the centre.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 15 s): Turn an inline formula into two 3D mirror-image structures.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: S3.2.7: draw stereochemical formulas showing the tetrahedral arrangement around a chiral carbon.
   - *textbook gap*: Books show a ready-drawn pair. Here you find the centre from an inline formula and place a bulky group correctly.
   - *avoid*: Drawing the structural formula twice with no 3D information.
   - *why*: The syllabus requires drawn tetrahedral stereochemical formulas for chiral carbons, so a flat structural formula does not meet the skill.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): The bulky C(CH3)3 is just one group on the centre.
   - *disguise*: An inline formula with a bracketed group hides which carbon is chiral.
   - *heuristic*: Expand it; only a carbon with one H can pass here.
   - *avoid*: Calling the C(CH3)3 carbon chiral; it has three identical methyl groups.
   - *why*: Chirality depends on four different attachments, not on their size, so a large group occupies one position like any other.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 10 s): Flat crosses and reflect-plus-swap pairs are out.
   - *definitely wrong*: A cross with 90° angles: The markscheme rejects 90° bond angles.; A mirror image with two groups also swapped: The drawing returns to the original molecule.; Centre placed on the C(CH3)3 carbon: Three identical methyl groups cannot give four different groups.
   - *avoid*: Checking only that the groups are correct, without comparing their positions.
   - *why*: A swap undoes a reflection, leaving two drawings of the same molecule rather than a pair of enantiomers.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 5 s): Wedge-dash drawing is not in the booklet sections provided.
   - *sections*: 
   - *pattern*: No table helps; this is a drawing routine.
   - *memorise*: The definition of a chiral carbon and wedge-dash conventions are NOT in the sections provided; memorise them.
   - *avoid*: Searching for a template during the exam.
   - *why*: The sections provided give spectral data. They do not show how to draw stereochemistry.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 60 s): Draw one carefully, then copy it in reverse.
   - *workflow*: Find the four-different carbon; Draw one tetrahedron; Reflect it
   - *fat to trim*: Show the C(CH3)3 as a group; do not draw every C-H bond.
   - *avoid*: Drawing the second isomer from scratch.
   - *why*: The second structure comes directly from the first, so reflecting is faster and safer than designing it again.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 10 s): Full marks need correct tetrahedral geometry shown with wedge-dash.
   - *trigger phrases*: wedge-dash; mirror images; correct bond angles
   - *trap boundary*: Omitting wedge-dash gives 1 max; 90° bond angles are not accepted.
   - *delta*: One mark: correct non-3D pair with proper angles. Two marks: the mirror-image pair also shows wedge-dash.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.2.HL.6221.4
   - *avoid*: Using 90° bond angles, or changing two groups as well as wedge-dash; both were reported in 25M.2.HL.6221.4.
   - *why*: The drawings must communicate 3D. Omitting wedge-dash caps the answer at one mark, even with sensible angles.

**Answer:** Two mirror-image tetrahedral wedge-dash structures centred on the CHBr carbon, with H, Br, CH3 and C(CH3)3 as its four groups. (Mirror one wedge-dash tetrahedron around the carbon with four different groups.)

## [ ] S3.2-05 · NMR ratios: a 6 means two equivalent methyl groups

B. Butan-2-ol

7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 5 s): One letter only: signals and ratio must both match.

   - *trap boundary*: A correct signal count alone is not enough without the exact ratio.
   - *delta*: Right answer: 6, 2, 1 and 1 hydrogens. Tempting butan-2-ol: five signals with no 6.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.1A.HL.6219.18

## [ ] S3.2-11 · Molecular ion: gives Mr, but is not always the tallest or most stable

Consider these statements about the molecular ion, M+.
I. It need not be the tallest peak in the spectrum.
II. It is always the most stable fragment produced.
III. Its m/z value gives the molecule's relative molecular mass.

B. I and III only
