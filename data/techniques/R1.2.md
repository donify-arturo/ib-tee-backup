## [ ] R1.2-01 · Bond enthalpy sums: broken minus formed, every bond counted
Priority 1 · R1.2.1 · Top examiner error signal in the pack ('bond enthalpy', 10 mentions). Calculations are often rated well prepared, yet sign and bond-count errors recur from 2006 onward.

**Step by step** (`18M.2.HL.TZ2.5`, 3 marks)

Hydrogen is made industrially from natural gas and steam: CH4(g) + H2O(g) → 3H2(g) + CO(g). Use average bond enthalpies from the data booklet, with C≡O taken as 1077 kJ mol−1, to find ΔH for this reaction in kJ.

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 5 s): Determine: one value, but each sum earns its own mark.
   - *command term*: Determine
   - *demands*: Reach the single value from bond data, showing both totals.
   - *score mechanics*: Three marks: one each for broken total, formed total and signed final value.
   - *avoid*: Writing just a final number: one slip then loses everything.
   - *why*: The final mark rewards only sign and subtraction; the sums score on their own.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 15 s): Standard marks-rich calculation, built from a booklet lookup.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: R1.2.1: calculate the enthalpy change of a reaction from average bond enthalpy data.
   - *textbook gap*: Books use small molecules with bonds drawn. Exams expect you to count bonds yourself from formulas.
   - *avoid*: Reaching for formation data here; part (b) asks for that separately.
   - *why*: Bonds are the only energy terms used, so each bond counted carries straight through to the answer.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): Industry story hides a plain broken-minus-formed sum.
   - *disguise*: An industrial hydrogen process with an unusual triple-bonded product, CO.
   - *heuristic*: If a bond table is called for, ignore the story and tally bonds.
   - *avoid*: Using C–O or C=O because CO looks like a carbonyl; it is given as triple.
   - *why*: Context changes nothing. Only the species and their bonds matter, and the given value replaces a lookup.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 10 s): A negative answer is out: broken clearly outweighs formed.
   - *definitely wrong*: a negative value: The rough broken total exceeds the rough formed total.; a value using product bonds only: Reactant bonds must break, so both sides must appear.
   - *avoid*: Assuming every reaction is exothermic; this one absorbs energy.
   - *why*: A rough tally, about 2600 against 2400, fixes the sign before any exact arithmetic.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 20 s): Section 12 gives all values; the subtraction rule you memorise.
   - *sections*: 12
   - *pattern*: Section 12: C–H 414, O–H 463, H–H 436, C≡O 1077 kJ mol−1.
   - *memorise*: Broken minus formed is NOT in the supplied section text.
   - *avoid*: Taking C–O (358) or C=O (804) instead of the triple bond value.
   - *why*: The booklet supplies numbers only, so the direction of subtraction is the whole memory load.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 100 s): Tally, sum twice, subtract: under two minutes.
   - *workflow*: List bonds per molecule; Multiply by coefficients; Broken minus formed; Attach the sign
   - *fat to trim*: Skip shapes, lone pairs and any comment on the reaction type.
   - *avoid*: Drawing full three-dimensional shapes; 06M.2.SL.TZ0.1 needs only the bond counts.
   - *why*: A written tally prevents the forgotten-bond error examiners report most often.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 20 s): Broken 2582, formed 2385, then the signed difference: three marks.
   - *trigger phrases*: 4(C–H) + 2(H–O); 3(H–H) + C≡O; broken minus formed, with sign
   - *trap boundary*: The last mark needs the right subtraction: a negative result cannot be correct here.
   - *delta*: Both sums right with the subtraction reversed misses the final mark. Right direction and sign earns all three.
   - *evidence ref*: 18M.2.HL.TZ2.5
   - *avoid*: Formed minus broken: 06N.2.SL.TZ0.2 and 12N.2.SL.TZ0.2 both report this reversal.
   - *why*: Each sum is its own mark, so visible working still scores if a later step slips.

**Answer:** Broken: 4(414) + 2(463) = 2582 kJ. Formed: 3(436) + 1077 = 2385 kJ. ΔH = 2582 − 2385 = +197 kJ. (ΔH equals bonds broken minus bonds formed, counting every bond.)

## [ ] R1.2-03 · Bond enthalpy equations: gas to gaseous atoms, one mole of bonds
Priority 1 · R1.2.1 · Among the lowest in the pack: 10.82% (15M.1.SL.TZ1.16) and 35% (09M.1.SL.TZ1.16). 'Gaseous state' appears 5 times as an error signal.

2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): Definition test written as an equation choice.

3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): Unfamiliar silane and a fraction hide a standard definition.

   - *heuristic*: Ask how many moles of the named bond break in each equation.
   - *avoid*: Rejecting B because a quarter of a molecule looks odd.

4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 15 s): A, C and D each fail one definition check.

5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 5 s): Section 12 lists Si–H; the definition you memorise.

   - *pattern*: Section 12 lists Si–H at 323 kJ mol−1.

   - *avoid*: Hunting the booklet for an equation; the supplied section gives only values.
   - *why*: The table gives a number, never the process it describes.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 30 s): Check products, then amount: thirty seconds.
   - *workflow*: Check gas; Check atoms; Count bonds broken
   - *fat to trim*: Skip the booklet's Si–H value: it does not affect the choice.
   - *avoid*: Calculating any value; no numbers are needed.
   - *why*: All species are gaseous here, so the state check removes nothing. Go to products and amount.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 15 s): Only one option has gas atoms and one mole of bonds.

## [ ] R1.2-04 · Hess's law from equations: flip, scale, then add
Priority 2 · R1.2.2 · 'Hess law' appears 7 times as an error signal. Correct rates range from 60% to 90%, with factor errors the named cause.

**Step by step** (`18N.1.HL.TZ0.14`, 1 marks)

Two reactions are given: Fe2O3(s) + CO(g) → 2FeO(s) + CO2(g), ΔH = −3 kJ, and Fe(s) + CO2(g) → FeO(s) + CO(g), ΔH = +11 kJ. Find ΔH, in kJ, for Fe2O3(s) + 3CO(g) → 2Fe(s) + 3CO2(g).
A. −25
B. −14
C. +8
D. +19

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 5 s): No command term: one letter, no method marks.
   - *command term*: none
   - *demands*: Choose the one correct enthalpy value.
   - *score mechanics*: One mark, all or nothing.
   - *avoid*: Trusting a matching option: the wrong factor also lands on one.
   - *why*: Working earns nothing in Paper 1A, so a quick sign and factor check protects the mark.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): Core Hess skill, set almost every year as an MCQ.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: R1.2.2: apply Hess's law to calculate enthalpy changes in multistep reactions.
   - *textbook gap*: Books show two-step cycles with neat numbers. Exams need one equation reversed and doubled at once.
   - *avoid*: Thinking this is a Born-Haber problem: 16M.1.SL.TZ0.14 notes the difference.
   - *why*: Enthalpy depends only on start and end states, so equations can be added like algebra.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): Unfamiliar iron oxides, but FeO just needs to cancel.
   - *disguise*: An iron oxide intermediate that never appears in the target.
   - *heuristic*: Find the species missing from the target and make it cancel.
   - *avoid*: Trying to use FeO's formation data; none is needed.

4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 10 s): Positive answers go: both contributions must be negative.

5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 1 s): Nothing to look up: every ΔH is in the stem.
   - *sections*:

   - *memorise*: Reversing changes the sign; multiplying scales ΔH. NOT in the booklet.
   - *avoid*: Looking up iron oxide values; they are not needed.
   - *why*: The method is pure manipulation, so there are no booklet values to read.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 50 s): Fix the flip and the factor: under a minute.
   - *workflow*: Flip the one against you; Scale to match the target; Add, then check it cancels
