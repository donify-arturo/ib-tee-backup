## [ ] R1.4-07 · Crossover temperature: set ΔG⊖ to zero
Priority 1 · R1.4.2, R1.4.3 · 2025 report calls this a good discriminator; most earned at least one mark.

**Step by step** (`25M.2.HL.TZ3.5`, 2 marks)

The hydrogenation C2H2(g) + 2H2(g) → C2H6(g) has ΔH⊖ = −312 kJ mol⁻¹ (from the previous part) and ΔS⊖ = −233 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Work out the temperature at which this reaction stops being spontaneous.

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 5 s): Calculate: the final value alone earns both marks.
   - *command term*: Calculate
   - *demands*: A temperature in K from thermodynamic values.
   - *score mechanics*: [2] for correct final answer; conversion setup earns M1.
   - *avoid*: Giving only a negative temperature and moving on.
   - *why*: Correct final answer gets both marks, but written working protects the first mark if the arithmetic slips.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): The switch point is where ΔG⊖ equals zero.
   - *ao level*: AO3
   - *syllabus hook*: R1.4.3: determine the temperature at which a reaction becomes spontaneous.
   - *textbook gap*: Books give the sign table; exams want a number from the same logic.
   - *avoid*: Trying to substitute 298 K for T.
   - *why*: Spontaneity depends on the sign of ΔG⊖, so the boundary between the regimes must be ΔG⊖ = 0.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 5 s): 'No longer spontaneous' and 'becomes spontaneous' use one equation.
   - *disguise*: Stem words: 'above which not spontaneous' or 'becomes spontaneous'.
   - *heuristic*: Any question about switching: set ΔG⊖ to zero.
   - *avoid*: Changing the method because the reaction is exothermic.
   - *why*: Both phrases describe the same boundary, so wording changes nothing in the calculation.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 5 s): Negative or tiny temperatures are slips.
   - *definitely wrong*: Any negative temperature: Kelvin temperature cannot be negative.; A value near 1 K: ΔS was left unconverted.
   - *avoid*: Writing down the negative value and hoping for follow-through marks.
   - *why*: Kelvin cannot be negative, and about 1.3 K shows that ΔS was never converted.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 5 s): Section 1 gives Gibbs; rearrange it yourself.
   - *sections*: 1
   - *pattern*: Section 1: ΔG⊖ = ΔH⊖ − TΔS⊖; set left side to zero.
   - *memorise*: T = ΔH⊖/ΔS⊖ is NOT printed; derive it.
   - *avoid*: Looking for a separate crossover formula.
   - *why*: The booklet gives only the general equation, and the crossover form comes from setting ΔG⊖ to zero.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 45 s): Set zero, convert, divide, sense-check.
   - *workflow*: Set ΔG to zero; Match kJ and J; T equals ΔH over ΔS
   - *fat to trim*: Skip converting to °C; kelvin is the answer.
   - *avoid*: Calculating ΔG⊖ at several temperatures.
   - *why*: Three moves give the answer and the check catches sign errors immediately.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 40 s): T = 312000/233 = 1340 K.
   - *trigger phrases*: 0 = ΔH⊖ − TΔS⊖; −233 × 10⁻³ kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; 1340 K or above
   - *trap boundary*: A negative Kelvin answer gets no error-carried-forward for the second mark.
   - *delta*: Partial credit for the converted setup; full credit needs the correct final temperature.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.2.HL.TZ3.5
   - *avoid*: Missing the conversion of ΔS or ΔH: the main mistake in the report (25M.2.HL.TZ3.5).
   - *why*: Both terms carry a negative sign, so they cancel and give a positive temperature.

**Answer:** 0 = (−312) − T(−0.233), so T = 1340 K (about 1.34 × 10³ K); above this temperature the reaction is non-spontaneous. (At the crossover ΔG⊖ = 0, so T = ΔH⊖/ΔS⊖.)

## [ ] R1.4-08 · Signs of ΔH and ΔS from temperature behaviour
Priority 1 · R1.4.3 · Strong: 53% correct in May 2025, one of the hardest items here.

**Step by step** (`25M.1A.HL.6219.24`, 1 marks)

A reaction does not proceed spontaneously when cold, but does when hot. Which pair of signs fits?
A. ΔH positive, ΔS negative
B. ΔH negative, ΔS negative
C. ΔH positive, ΔS positive
D. ΔH negative, ΔS positive

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 3 s): MCQ on a sign pair: reason, then pick.
   - *command term*: none
   - *demands*: Select the sign pair matching the behaviour.
   - *score mechanics*: One mark, all or nothing.
   - *avoid*: Locking in one sign and picking by feel.
   - *why*: Both signs must be right, so half-knowledge is exactly what the distractors reward.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): Two temperature limits fix both signs.
   - *ao level*: AO3
   - *syllabus hook*: R1.4.3: interpret the sign of ΔG; a change is spontaneous if ΔG is negative.
   - *textbook gap*: Books print a four-row table; exams reword it so a memorised table can misfire.
   - *avoid*: Relying on a memorised table; reports link rote learning to wrong answers (05M.1.SL.TZ0.18).
   - *why*: As T approaches zero the entropy term vanishes, and at large T it dominates, so each limit fixes one sign.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 5 s): Sometimes a real reaction replaces the verbal description.
   - *disguise*: A named reaction, such as CaCO3 decomposing, given with behaviour.
   - *heuristic*: Translate words to the limits: cold means ΔH; hot means −TΔS.
   - *avoid*: Assuming every decomposition or combustion behaves the same.
   - *why*: An equation lets you find ΔS from gas moles, leaving ΔH to follow from the behaviour.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 10 s): Opposite-sign rows never switch with temperature.
   - *definitely wrong*: A: ΔG is positive at every temperature.; B: Would be spontaneous when cold, not hot.; D: ΔG is negative at every temperature.
   - *avoid*: Choosing B; ΔH negative makes the reaction spontaneous when cold.
   - *why*: With opposite signs both terms pull ΔG the same way, so its sign is fixed at every temperature.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 3 s): Section 1 has the equation; reasoning does the rest.
   - *sections*: 1
   - *pattern*: Section 1: ΔG⊖ = ΔH⊖ − TΔS⊖.
   - *memorise*: The four-case sign table is NOT in the booklet.
   - *avoid*: Looking for a table of sign combinations.
   - *why*: The Gibbs equation is printed, and both limits follow directly from it.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 25 s): Cold sets ΔH; hot sets ΔS.
   - *workflow*: Cold: ΔG takes ΔH's sign; Hot: ΔG takes −ΔS's sign; Pick the row
   - *fat to trim*: Skip trial values.
   - *avoid*: Substituting invented numbers into the Gibbs equation.
   - *why*: Two short deductions, one from each limit, select a single row.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 20 s): Cold non-spontaneous means ΔH +; hot spontaneous means ΔS +.
   - *trigger phrases*: both entropy and enthalpy change positive; non-spontaneous at low temperatures; spontaneous at higher temperatures
   - *trap boundary*: When both are negative, the sign of ΔG cannot be stated without the temperature (05M.1.SL.TZ0.18).
   - *delta*: Only C has a positive ΔH and a positive ΔS.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.1A.HL.6219.24
   - *avoid*: Reversing the sign of ΔS by forgetting the minus in −TΔS.
   - *why*: Positive ΔH makes ΔG positive when cold; positive ΔS eventually makes −TΔS large enough to win.

**Answer:** C. Positive ΔH makes ΔG positive at low T; positive ΔS makes −TΔS dominate at high T, so ΔG becomes negative. (Low T: ΔH decides. High T: −TΔS decides.)

A. I and II only
B. I and III only

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 3 s): Multiple-statement MCQ: judge each statement alone.

   - *avoid*: Searching section 13 for CaCO3.
