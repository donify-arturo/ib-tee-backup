## [ ] R2.2-04 · Units of k come from the overall order, not from the number of species
Priority 1 · R2.2.11, R2.2.10 · Under 43% correct on 25M.1A.HL.6219.28. 'Rate constant' is the second most frequent examiner error phrase (n=10), and unit errors recur from 2004 to 2024.

**Step by step** (`25M.1A.HL.6219.28`, 1 marks)

A reaction has the rate equation rate = k[X]^2[Y]^0. What are the units of k?
A. mol2 dm−6 s−1
B. mol dm−3 s−1
C. mol−1 dm3 s−1
D. mol−2 dm6 s−1

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 10 s): MCQ: work the units out instead of recalling them.
   - *command term*: none
   - *demands*: Choose k's units for an overall order of 2.
   - *score mechanics*: 1 mark, all or nothing.
   - *avoid*: Picking whichever unit looks familiar.
   - *why*: None of the distractors stood out, so students were guessing (25M.1A.HL.6219.28). Working it out removes the guess.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): AO2: work out units from the overall order.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: R2.2.11: k depends on temperature, and its units are determined by the overall order.
   - *textbook gap*: Books give a table of units. Exams make you work them out, often with a zero-order term shown.
   - *avoid*: Treating [Y]^0 as if Y were first order.
   - *why*: R2.2.11 says k's units depend on the overall order. The question tests whether you can do that unit algebra.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): The [Y]^0 term tempts you to count Y.
   - *disguise*: A zero-order term is written in explicitly as a distractor.
   - *heuristic*: Overall order = sum of exponents; a zero counts as zero.
   - *avoid*: Counting the species in the rate equation instead of adding the exponents.
   - *why*: Anything raised to the power 0 equals 1 and has no units, so Y does not enter the overall order.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 10 s): Rule out A, B and D without any algebra.
   - *definitely wrong*: A: A positive power of mol would need overall order −1, which is impossible.; B: mol dm−3 s−1 is the unit of rate, so it fits only a zero-order k.; D: mol−2 dm6 s−1 needs an overall order of 3.
   - *avoid*: Picking D because X is squared.
   - *why*: Each wrong option matches a different order (−1, 0 or 3), never 2.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 5 s): Units of k are not in the booklet.
   - *sections*: 
   - *pattern*: None.
   - *memorise*: k units = mol^(1−n) dm^(3n−3) s−1 for overall order n.
   - *avoid*: Searching the booklet for units of k.
   - *why*: The booklet lists equations and constants, not rate-constant units.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 20 s): Overall order 2 gives mol−1 dm3 s−1.
   - *workflow*: Add up the exponents; Apply the 1 − n rule; Check signs are opposite
   - *fat to trim*: No dimensional working needed once the 0–3 pattern is known.
   - *avoid*: Writing out the full division when the pattern is already known.
   - *why*: Once the order-to-units pattern is learnt, this takes seconds.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 15 s): C: mol−1 dm3 s−1.
   - *trigger phrases*: mol−1 dm3 s−1 for overall second order; mol−2 dm6 s−1 for overall third order (24N.2.HL.TZ0.6)
   - *trap boundary*: In written questions, wrong or missing units lose the units mark (08M.2.HL.TZ0.7).
   - *delta*: C versus D: D is for third order. C correctly gives the zero-order term no units.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.1A.HL.6219.28
   - *avoid*: Guessing from memory: fewer than 43% were right (25M.1A.HL.6219.28 report).
   - *why*: Rate is always mol dm−3 s−1. Dividing by (mol dm−3)^2 leaves one mol dm−3 on the bottom, which gives mol−1 dm3 s−1.

**Answer:** C. The overall order is 2, so k = (mol dm−3 s−1) ÷ (mol dm−3)^2 = mol−1 dm3 s−1. (Units of k = (mol dm−3)^(1−n) s−1, where n is the overall order.)

- *ao*: AO2

- *syllabus hook*: R2.2.11: solve rate-equation problems, including the units of k.
- *textbook gap*: Books show a table of units. Exams ask for k's value and units together after a rate-data question.

**2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases**

  - mol−2 dm6 s−1 for third order (24N.2.HL.TZ0.6)

- *delta*: The value alone earns partial credit; the value with correct units earns full credit.

**3 · Novel application**

  - Time measured in minutes or hours (05N.2.HL.TZ0.1).

  - Apply 1 − n
  - Use the question's time unit

**6 · Data booklet**

**7 · Instant elimination**

  - mol dm−3 s−1 is the unit of k only for zero order.
  - s−1 on its own means first order.

## [ ] R2.2-05 · Rate equation from a mechanism: slow step, then replace the intermediate
Priority 1 · R2.2.6, R2.2.9, R2.2.10 · Examiner findings flag 'deducing the rate equation from a mechanism' as difficult in May 2025, May 2019, May 2014, Nov 2014 and Nov 2012. About 75% succeed when the slow step is first.

**Step by step** (`25M.2.HL.6221.8`, 2 marks)

An exothermic reaction between A and B happens in two steps:
Step 1 (fast): A + B → C + D
Step 2 (slow): D + B → E
Deduce the rate equation for the reaction. [2]

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 10 s): Deduce: reason from the steps to the rate equation.
   - *command term*: Deduce
   - *demands*: The rate equation, with the orders that follow from the mechanism.
   - *score mechanics*: 2 marks: the orders, then the equation. A correct equation earns both.
   - *avoid*: Guessing orders from the overall equation.
   - *why*: Deduce means reaching a conclusion from the information given: the steps and which one is slow.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): AO3: rate law when the slow step comes second.
   - *ao level*: AO3
   - *syllabus hook*: R2.2.6: the slowest step determines the rate; mechanisms must be consistent with kinetic data.
   - *textbook gap*: Books mostly show mechanisms whose slow step is first. Exams put the slow step second, so you must replace the intermediate.
   - *avoid*: Writing rate = k[D][B]: D is an intermediate.
   - *why*: R2.2.6 guidance asks for examples where the rate-determining step is not the first step. That case is exactly what is tested here.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 15 s): Letters, not real formulas, hide which species is the intermediate.
   - *disguise*: Abstract letters A–E with no chemistry to lean on.
   - *heuristic*: Made, then used = intermediate, so it never appears in the rate equation.
   - *avoid*: Putting C into the rate equation.
   - *why*: D is made in step 1 and used in step 2, so it is the intermediate. C and E are never used again, so they are the products (part (a)(i)).
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 5 s): Any rate equation containing D, C or E is wrong.
   - *definitely wrong*: A rate equation containing D: D is an intermediate.; A rate equation containing C or E: Products never appear in the rate equation.; Zero order in A: A forms D, which feeds the slow step.
   - *avoid*: Keeping D because it appears in the slow step.
   - *why*: Intermediates and products never appear in a rate equation built from the starting reactants.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 5 s): The booklet has nothing on mechanisms.
   - *sections*: 
   - *pattern*: None.
   - *memorise*: The slowest step sets the rate. Intermediates are replaced by the reactants that formed them.
   - *avoid*: Looking for rate equations in section 1.
   - *why*: Rate-determining-step rules are not in the booklet.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 45 s): Slow step, swap D, add the powers of B.
   - *workflow*: Find the slow step; Replace the intermediate; Add the repeated powers
   - *fat to trim*: Skip any working with K for the fast step.
   - *avoid*: Deriving an equilibrium constant for the fast step.
   - *why*: Three moves give the answer. No equilibrium algebra is needed at IB level.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 20 s): A is first order, B second order: rate = k[A][B]^2.
   - *trigger phrases*: A: first order AND B: second order; «rate =» k[A][B]^2
   - *trap boundary*: A rate equation that contains an intermediate (D) or a product scores nothing.
   - *delta*: k[A][B] counts B only once. k[A][B]^2 counts B in both steps.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.2.HL.6221.8
   - *avoid*: Stopping at the slow step's species; this part drew comment from teachers (25M.2.HL.6221.8 report) and was flagged difficult in May 2025.
   - *why*: M1 is both orders and M2 is the full equation. A correct equation on its own scores [2].

**Answer:** rate = k[A][B]^2. The slow step uses D and B, and D comes from A + B in the fast step, so the reaction is first order in A and second order in B. (The slow step sets the rate; replace intermediate D with what forms it.)
**Try next:** `25N.1A.HL.6219.26`, `24N.1.HL.TZ0.21`

**1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge**
- *ao*: AO3
- *papers*:  - 1A
  - 2
- *syllabus hook*: R2.2.6 and R2.2.9: the rate-determining step; the rate equation depends on the mechanism.
- *textbook gap*: Books show slow-first examples. Exams use a fast step before the slow one, or ask you to judge whether a mechanism fits a given rate equation.

**3 · Novel application**

**5 · Time efficiency**

- *fat to trim*: No equilibrium-constant algebra is needed.

**6 · Data booklet**
- *sections*:  - —
- *hack*: Mechanism rules and rate equations are NOT in the booklet.

**7 · Instant elimination**
- *rules*:  - Species that react only in steps after the slow step are zero order.
  - Intermediates and products never appear in the rate equation.
  - A species that appears in both the fast and the slow step has its powers added.

## [ ] R2.2-09 · Arrhenius plot: ln k against 1/T has gradient −Ea/R; convert J to kJ
Priority 1 · R2.2.12, R2.2.13 · 78% on the gradient MCQ, but graphical Ea determination was flagged difficult in Nov 2011, Nov 2012 and May 2018. 'Activation energy' is the top examiner error phrase (n=17).

**Step by step** (`24N.1.HL.TZ0.20`, 1 marks)

Rate constants measured at several temperatures were plotted as ln k against 1/T, giving a straight line. The linear Arrhenius equation is ln k = −Ea/(RT) + ln A. What does the gradient of the line equal?
A. −Ea/R
B. −Ea
C. ln A
D. −Ea/RT

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 10 s): MCQ: identify the gradient expression.
   - *command term*: none
   - *demands*: Choose what the gradient of ln k against 1/T represents.
   - *score mechanics*: 1 mark, all or nothing.
   - *avoid*: Picking the option with the most symbols.
   - *why*: A one-step recognition of the gradient, but the distractors are close.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): AO2: match the linear Arrhenius form to y = mx + c.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: R2.2.12: the Arrhenius equation and its linear form are used to find Ea from graphs.
   - *textbook gap*: Books derive the linear form. Exams give a numerical gradient and ask for Ea with R and a kJ conversion.
   - *avoid*: Memorising 'gradient = Ea' without the minus sign and R.
   - *why*: R2.2.12 asks you to analyse the linear form of the Arrhenius equation. The booklet gives the equation; you have to read the gradient from it.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): The equation looks complicated, but it is just a straight line.
   - *disguise*: A numerical gradient, such as −6400, with options already in kJ (25M.1A.HL.6207.31).
   - *heuristic*: Whatever is in front of x is the gradient.
   - *avoid*: Treating the R and T together as one constant.
   - *why*: Whatever multiplies x is the gradient; whatever stands alone is the intercept.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 10 s): B, C and D fail on units or role.
   - *definitely wrong*: B: No R: the units cannot match a gradient measured in K.; C: ln A is the y-intercept, not the gradient.; D: It contains T, but T is on the x-axis, not a constant.
   - *avoid*: Picking C because ln A is in the equation.
   - *why*: The gradient of ln k against 1/T has units of K, which only −Ea/R gives.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 10 s): Section 1 gives both Arrhenius forms; section 2 gives R.
   - *sections*: 1; 2
   - *pattern*: Section 1: k = Ae^(−Ea/RT) and ln k = −Ea/RT + ln A. Section 2: R = 8.31 J K−1 mol−1.
   - *memorise*: Gradient = −Ea/R and intercept = ln A; the booklet does not state these.
   - *avoid*: Using R = 8.31 and forgetting the result is in J, not kJ.
   - *why*: The booklet gives the equations and R = 8.31 J K−1 mol−1, so only the matching to y = mx + c is left.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 15 s): Write y = mx + c beside the equation and read off m.
   - *workflow*: Write y = mx + c; x is 1/T; m = −Ea/R
   - *fat to trim*: No need to take logs yourself: the linear form is in the booklet.
   - *avoid*: Deriving the equation from k = Ae^(−Ea/RT).
   - *why*: Lining up the terms takes seconds and removes the ambiguity.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 15 s): A: the gradient is −Ea/R.
   - *trigger phrases*: gradient = −Ea/R; x-axis: 1/T AND y-axis: ln k (25M.2.HL.6215.3)
   - *trap boundary*: Written items accept the gradient within a range (1.4–1.5 × 10^3 K in 25M.2.HL.6215.3); it must be negative.
   - *delta*: A versus D: whether you saw that 1/T is the x variable, not a constant.
   - *evidence ref*: 24N.1.HL.TZ0.20
   - *avoid*: Option D, which keeps T inside the gradient; about 22% missed this (24N.1.HL.TZ0.20 report: 78% correct).
   - *why*: x is 1/T, so T is already part of the x variable. Only −Ea/R multiplies x.

**Answer:** A. The gradient equals −Ea/R; the y-intercept is ln A. (ln k against 1/T has gradient −Ea/R and intercept ln A.)

## [ ] R2.2-01 · Rate factors: pressure only counts for gaseous reactants; explain with collisions per unit time
Priority 2 · R2.2.2, R2.2.3, R2.2.4 · 51% correct on 25M.1A.HL.6213.25, with 30% wrongly including pressure. 'Factors affecting rate', 'effect of pressure' and 'connecting rate explanation with time' were flagged difficult in May 2025.

**Step by step** (`25M.1A.HL.6213.25`, 1 marks)

Aqueous hydrogen peroxide decomposes: 2H2O2(aq) → 2H2O(l) + O2(g). Which changes make this reaction faster?
I. Adding a catalyst
II. Using a more concentrated H2O2(aq) solution
III. Raising the pressure
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 10 s): MCQ: judge each numbered statement on its own.
   - *command term*: none
   - *demands*: Decide for this reaction whether each of I, II and III increases the rate.
   - *score mechanics*: 1 mark, all or nothing.
   - *avoid*: Picking 'all three' because each factor sounds familiar.
   - *why*: In I/II/III options, one false statement removes every option that contains it. Judging the statements one by one is faster than comparing whole options.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): AO2: apply the rate factors to one stated reaction.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: R2.2.3: pressure, concentration, surface area, temperature and catalysts influence rate.
   - *textbook gap*: Books teach pressure using all-gas examples. The exam hides a solution reaction that gives off a gaseous product.
   - *avoid*: Treating the syllabus list of factors as true for every reaction.
   - *why*: R2.2.3 lists pressure as a factor, but it works only by raising the concentration of gas particles. The exam checks whether you test that against this reaction.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): The gaseous product O2 tempts you into picking pressure.
   - *disguise*: A gas appears in the equation, but only on the product side.
   - *heuristic*: Ask which reactant particles get closer together when the pressure rises.
   - *avoid*: Reasoning that 'there is a gas in the equation', so pressure matters.
   - *why*: Rate depends on collisions between reactant particles. O2 is only formed, so squeezing it does nothing to how often H2O2 particles collide.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 5 s): III is false, which removes B, C and D.
   - *definitely wrong*: B: Contains III, but no reactant is a gas.; C: Contains III, but no reactant is a gas.; D: Contains III, but no reactant is a gas.
   - *avoid*: Keeping D because I and II are both correct.
   - *why*: Every option except A includes pressure, so one check settles it.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 5 s): The booklet does not help; the state symbols are the data.
   - *sections*: 
   - *pattern*: None. Read the state symbols in the given equation.
   - *memorise*: The five rate factors, and that pressure acts only through the concentration of a gaseous reactant.
   - *avoid*: Searching the booklet for rate factors.
   - *why*: Collision theory and rate factors are not in the data booklet.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 15 s): Check states first, then strike out III.
   - *workflow*: Read the reactant state symbols; Strike pressure if no gas; Only A lacks III
   - *fat to trim*: No need to think further about I or II once III is out.
   - *avoid*: Weighing up I and II at length when III decides it.
   - *why*: Only option A leaves out III, so once pressure is ruled out the answer is fixed.
