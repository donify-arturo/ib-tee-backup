## [ ] R3.4-01 · SN2 mechanism: four marks for exact arrows, not just the idea
Priority 1 · R3.4.2, R3.4.3, R3.4.9 · Curly arrows are the most flagged error in this sub-topic. In 2025 the mechanism discriminated strongly, with low scorers struggling on every part (25M.2.HL.6221.4).

**Step by step** (`25M.2.HL.6221.4`, 4 marks)

Bromoalkane X is CH3CHBrC(CH3)3. It reacts with sodium hydroxide, and the reaction depends on the concentrations of both reactants. Using curly arrows to show how the electrons move, explain the mechanism of this reaction. [4]

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 5 s): Explain here means draw the mechanism, not write sentences.
   - *command term*: Explain
   - *demands*: Show how electron pairs move through the reaction, using curly arrows on a drawn mechanism.
   - *score mechanics*: One mark per feature: first arrow, second arrow, transition state, products. Capped at 3 for SN1.
   - *avoid*: Naming the mechanism only; 14N.3.HL.TZ0.10 reports candidates stating names instead of outlining mechanisms.
   - *why*: The markscheme credits features of the drawing, so prose about collisions or rates earns no extra marks.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 15 s): Secondary bromide; the rate clue points to SN2.
   - *ao level*: AO3
   - *syllabus hook*: R3.4.9: primary SN2, tertiary SN1, both for secondary; describe and explain the mechanisms.
   - *textbook gap*: Books show SN2 on a small primary molecule. This question uses a bulky secondary bromide and hides the choice in the stem.
   - *avoid*: Deciding from structure alone: secondary allows either mechanism, and an SN1 answer caps at 3 marks here.
   - *why*: Secondary halogenoalkanes can react by both mechanisms, so structure alone cannot decide. The concentration clue does.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): Bulky X looks odd, but only one carbon matters.
   - *disguise*: A bulky C(CH3)3 group replaces the usual small primary bromide.
   - *heuristic*: Circle C–Br, call the rest R, then draw OH⁻ attacking that carbon.
   - *avoid*: Miscopying the C(CH3)3 end; examiners saw wrong organic products on this question (25M.2.HL.6221.4).
   - *why*: The mechanism happens at the carbon holding Br, so the rest of the molecule is carried through unchanged as an R group.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 5 s): Rule out carbocations, H-started arrows and Na–O bonds at sight.
   - *definitely wrong*: A carbocation intermediate: Rate depends on both reactants, so this is one step with no intermediate.; An arrow starting at H of OH⁻: The electron pair comes from O, not H.; A covalent Na–O or Na–Br bond: These sodium compounds are ionic.
   - *avoid*: Drawing NaOH or NaBr with covalent bonds, which examiners reported here (25M.2.HL.6221.4).
   - *why*: Each contradicts the one-step electron-pair picture or ionic bonding in sodium compounds, so no analysis is needed.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 2 s): Nothing here is in the booklet; memorise the drawing.
   - *sections*: 
   - *pattern*: None: no table helps here.
   - *memorise*: Arrow start and end points and the transition state form are NOT in the booklet.
   - *avoid*: Hunting through the booklet for mechanisms during the paper.
   - *why*: Mechanism conventions are a skill, not data, so no table can replace drawing them.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 120 s): Four drawing moves, about two minutes.
   - *workflow*: Lone pair to carbon; Bond to bromine; Brackets, partial bonds, minus charge; Alcohol plus Br⁻
   - *fat to trim*: No explanation of rates, no 180° perfection, no drawing of Na⁺.
   - *avoid*: Drawing the other mechanism as well; 03M.2.HL.TZ0.5 reports answers giving both.
   - *why*: A fixed drawing order stops you missing the charge or Br⁻, parts examiners often report as missing (18N.2.HL.TZ0.9).
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 20 s): Four marks: two arrows, transition state, products.
   - *trigger phrases*: arrow from lone pair/negative charge on O to C bonded to Br; arrow from C–Br bond to Br; negative charge AND partial bonds; Br⁻ AND CH3CH(OH)C(CH3)3
   - *trap boundary*: An SN1 mechanism caps at 3 marks. NaBr as a product counts only if Na⁺ was shown at the start.
   - *delta*: Full credit needs both transition-state features and both products. Partial bonds without the charge, or a missing Br⁻, loses that mark.
   - *evidence ref*: 25M.2.HL.6221.4
   - *avoid*: Arrow from the H of OH⁻, or from C instead of the C–Br bond, both reported repeatedly (11M.2.HL.TZ2.8, 17M.2.HL.TZ2.7).
   - *why*: Each mark checks a separate feature of electron movement, so a correct name alone earns nothing and one slip costs only one mark.

**Answer:** SN2. Curly arrow from the lone pair or negative charge on O of OH⁻ to the C bonded to Br. Curly arrow from the C–Br bond to Br. Transition state with an overall negative charge and partial bonds to O and Br. Products: CH3CH(OH)C(CH3)3 and Br⁻. (Rate depends on both reactants, so SN2: one step, two arrows, one charged transition state.)

## [ ] R3.4-02 · Classify the carbon first: tertiary means SN1 and a one-term rate
Priority 2 · R3.4.9, R3.4.10 · 60% correct in November 2024 and about 32% in May 2015 on tertiary rate questions.

**Step by step** (`24N.1.HL.TZ0.36`, 1 marks)

2-bromo-2-methylbutane, CH3CH2CBr(CH3)2, is warmed with sodium hydroxide solution and an alcohol forms. Which option gives the mechanism and the rate expression?
A. SN2; rate = k[CH3CH2CBr(CH3)2][OH⁻]
B. SN1; rate = k[CH3CH2CBr(CH3)2]
C. SN1; rate = k[CH3CH2CBr(CH3)2][OH⁻]
D. SN2; rate = k[CH3CH2CBr(CH3)2]

1. **4 · Command terms & score mechanics** (about 3 s): MCQ: no command term, one pairing to identify.
   - *command term*: none
   - *demands*: Choose the option where mechanism and rate expression are both right.
   - *score mechanics*: One mark, all or nothing; both columns must be correct.
   - *avoid*: Writing out the whole mechanism; the option needs only its type.
   - *why*: One mark depends on one correct pairing, so speed comes from classifying, not writing.
2. **1 · Syllabus-to-exam bridge** (about 10 s): Warm NaOH with a bromide: classify before reading options.
   - *ao level*: AO2
   - *syllabus hook*: R3.4.9: primary SN2, tertiary SN1; distinguish the one-step and two-step routes.
   - *textbook gap*: Books teach the two mechanisms separately. The exam pairs mechanism with rate expression, so structure and kinetics must be linked in one move.
   - *avoid*: Using OH⁻ in the reaction as proof it appears in the rate; in SN1 it reacts after the slow step.
   - *why*: The structure fixes the mechanism, and the mechanism fixes the rate expression, so one classification answers both columns.
3. **3 · Novel application** (about 10 s): The long name hides a simple tertiary carbon.
   - *disguise*: An ethyl group and a methyl in the name make the carbon look less substituted.
   - *heuristic*: Ignore the name: count carbons directly bonded to the C–Br carbon.
   - *avoid*: Treating the ethyl as part of the main chain and calling the carbon secondary.
   - *why*: Only substitution at the C–Br carbon matters: two methyls and one ethyl make three alkyl groups, whatever the name suggests.
4. **7 · Instant elimination** (about 8 s): Three options go without calculating: only one pair fits.
   - *definitely wrong*: C: An SN1 rate cannot include [OH⁻]; OH⁻ joins after the slow step.; D: An SN2 rate must include both reactants.; A: A tertiary carbon reacts by SN1, not SN2.
   - *avoid*: Treating C as safe because it says SN1; check its rate too.
   - *why*: C and D pair a mechanism with the wrong rate, and A gives SN2 to a tertiary carbon.
5. **6 · Data booklet** (about 2 s): Not in the booklet; memorise the class-to-mechanism link.
   - *sections*: 
   - *pattern*: None: no table helps here.
   - *memorise*: Primary SN2, tertiary SN1, secondary both; SN1 and SN2 rate expressions. All NOT in the booklet.
   - *avoid*: Searching the booklet for rate expressions.
   - *why*: The link comes from carbocation stability and crowding, which is reasoning rather than data.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 30 s): Classify, then cross out mismatches: under a minute.
   - *workflow*: Count alkyls on C–Br carbon; Tertiary goes SN1; Cross out mismatched pairs
   - *fat to trim*: Skip drawing the carbocation or the product.
   - *avoid*: Looking for rate data; none is given, so no calculation is possible.
   - *why*: Checking the pairs removes two options at once, leaving one structural decision.
7. **2 · Markscheme logic & trigger phrases** (about 15 s): Mechanism and rate must match: SN1, halogenoalkane only.
   - *trigger phrases*: SN1; rate = k[halogenoalkane]
   - *trap boundary*: The right mechanism with the wrong rate scores nothing: one option must fit both columns.
   - *delta*: C has SN1 right but adds [OH⁻]; the correct option keeps only the halogenoalkane, as the SN1 slow step requires.
   - *evidence ref*: 24N.1.HL.TZ0.36
   - *avoid*: Adding OH⁻ to an SN1 rate; only about 32% were correct on a similar tertiary rate question (15M.1.HL.TZ1.36).
   - *why*: Only the slow step sets the rate. In SN1 that step is C–Br breaking alone, so OH⁻ does not affect the rate.

## [ ] R3.4-04 · Benzene's electron-rich ring needs an electron-poor cation, not an anion

Benzene undergoes a substitution reaction with an ion. Which ion could act as the attacking species?
A. NO2⁻
B. NH4⁺

   - *command term*: none
   - *demands*: Choose the one ion that can attack benzene.
   - *score mechanics*: One mark, all or nothing.

   - *pattern*: None: no table helps here.

   - *avoid*: Searching the booklet for reacting species.
   - *why*: Electrophile identity is reaction knowledge, not a data table entry.
6. **5 · Time efficiency** (about 20 s): Charge check, then room check: about twenty seconds.
   - *workflow*: Cross out the anions; Is the cation electron-poor?; Pick the acceptor
   - *fat to trim*: Skip mixed-acid equations and mechanism steps.

   - *evidence ref*: 25M.1A.HL.6219.40
